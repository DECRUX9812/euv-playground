Demo 01 · Optics

Mo/Si multilayer mirror reflectivity

EUV has no lenses — the optics are Bragg mirrors: 10–80 alternating Mo/Si bilayer pairs (period d ≈ 6.9 nm, Γ = Mo fraction) that coherently reflect 13.5 nm light. Reflectivity is computed with the Parratt recursion (exact Fresnel stack treatment), including interface roughness (Debye–Waller) and absorption. Drag the sliders and watch the resonance move.

Demo 02 · Statistics

Photon shot noise — why EUV scaling is in trouble

Each 13.5 nm photon carries 91.8 eV, so an EUV exposure uses only a handful of photons per feature. Photon arrivals are Poisson — relative noise σ/μ = 1/√N. Fewer photons per pixel ⇒ noisier image ⇒ line-edge roughness you cannot engineer away.

(13.5 nm photons carry ~14× more energy than 193 nm ArF photons, which is precisely why the photon count is so low.)
Context

Why EUV is weird

  • No lenses — vacuum only. At 13.5 nm every material absorbs, so refractive optics are impossible. The scanner is an all-mirror, all-vacuum machine; the source is a laser-heated tin plasma.
  • Mirrors are Mo/Si multilayers. Each "mirror" is 40–60 alternating Mo/Si pairs (period ≈ 6.9 nm) engineered to Bragg-reflect at 13.5 nm. Peak reflectivity is only ~70% (vs 99.9% for visible light), so a 10-mirror optical train throws away ~99% of the source light.
  • Shot noise is brutal. 92 eV photons → ~20 photons per 10 nm feature at a 30 mJ/cm² dose → ±22% Poisson noise. Roughly a fifth of the "ink" writing a transistor is random chance.
